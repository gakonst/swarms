# Swarms

A community-funded science project on the Tempo gateway (https://gateway.tempo-dev.workers.dev): anyone starts a research swarm with a question and evidence requirements, funds it by sending it USDC.e, pathUSD, OUSD or MACH, and can contribute to an existing swarm or lend a machine. Swarm World makes the research visible. It uses only the gateway's public HTTP API, as any outside developer would, so it also tests that API.

## Research experience

- The home page (`Home.tsx`) is two big boxes, each one link (the user's request of 2026-11-02: two big buttons for a newcomer, not a list): Start a breakthrough (how many ideas wait, and up to four open ideas as small white cards) opens `/new`, and Be part of the future (how many are live or open, and up to four swarms as small cards with their raised amount) opens `/join`. The cards are a preview, not targets; phones show two per box. The lists with tap-to-expand rows live on `/join` and `/new`. `/about` (`About.tsx`, lazy, linked from the header and a swarm's About tab) says what Swarms is in five rows (Start, Fuel, Work, Steer, Finish), three plain facts (public, reported not reviewed, no returns) and links to start, join and the docs. Step headings carry no subtitles; the New flow's steps are Start, Fuel, Launch.
- The idea bank (`problems` in `shared/ResearchBrief.ts`, the user's request of 2026-11-02) is a curated list of problems, each with an `id`, a short label and hint, a title and a full evidence brief. `open(swarms)` drops every problem whose title matches (case-insensitively) a listed swarm that is not done, so the Pick step, the Start door and the `/x` card offer only problems nobody is working on; a finished swarm frees its problem again. The Pick step shows six at a time with More ideas, `/new?q=<id>` opens one on the Fuel step, and when every problem is taken it points to Join. A swarm stores the problem it was started from in `swarms.problem` (migration 0010, backfilled by exact title for older swarms), set by `POST /v1/swarms` and `swarm_create` with `problem` (one of the bank's ids, 400 otherwise) and exposed on `Summary.problem`; the Pick step and the `/x` card send it, a custom idea sends none, and `open` matches on it, so editing the title does not free a problem. Checked locally on 2026-11-02: 0010 applied, a swarm created with `problem: "sat-solver"` answered it back, and an unknown id answered 400.
- Design principle (the user's, 2026-11-02): avoid explicit buttons where the row or card itself can be the click target. The home doors, the join tiles, the idea cards, the notes under Back it (the row backs that note, with a + affordance), the artifact announcements in the room, the swarm's status callout (the whole strip opens Back it or the result), the empty transcript (opens Back it), the empty and offline join list, and the not-found and offline pages (rows: Try again, Join one, Start one) follow it. The join list is compact rows (status dot, one-line title, raised; the status words, area and chevron were dropped at the user's request), about 46 px each, in one column up to 760 px wide (scrolling inside its box on a phone), instead of 168 px cards. They are sorted by status and then creation time, so rows do not jump on each poll. One row is open at a time: the first tap expands it (full title, goal, the latest report's first plain line, what is left, backers, last activity), a tap on another row moves the expansion there, and a tap on the open row goes to the swarm. The New flow's Pick step is the same list (the user's request of 2026-11-02): one row per open idea with its mark and label, More ideas and Your own idea as the last rows; the first tap expands an idea into its hint and its brief in an editable box (the user's request: a template's prompt must be editable), with Fuel it; a second tap on the row also moves to Fuel with the edited brief, and opening another idea replaces the draft with that idea's brief; and Your own idea expands into the question box with Fuel it. Rows sit flush in one bordered group with dividers and no gaps (the user's request of 2026-11-02): the join list, the door ideas and peek, the idea list, the artifact list, the not-found rows, the More sheet and the room roster.
- The app has no footer and no "Demo data" line (the user's request of 2026-11-02). Desktop pages are centred: the two doors are a 640 px-high block centred in the window, Join's list is a 760 px column, New and Docs keep their widths centred with a top offset that grows with the window's height, and not-found pages fill the space under the header and centre in it. `/docs` is reached from `d`, the X cards and links; nothing in the header points to it.
- Funding is one component, `Fund` in `Contribute.tsx`, and one kind of payment (below): a dollar amount with $2, $5 and $10 and one button (Pay $2.00, or Launch · $2.00 on New) that sends a `transferWithMemo` of OUSD, USDC.e or pathUSD from Tempo Wallet to the swarm's wallet, or in MACH opens a channel the gateway spends for the swarm (below), and counts it (`send`, then `POST …/contributions`); a note behind the money shows as a removable chip. A swarm page opens it as a sheet from the head's Fund button, the callout, the empty transcript and the room (a phone sheet from the bottom, a centred card on a desktop). New uses the same component as its second step (Start, Fund). Errors and confirmations are `Alert` cards (`Alert.tsx`: a mark, a headline, an optional detail, an optional action), with `explain` turning wallet and network errors into plain headlines. The token is picked by `choose`: the first of OUSD, USDC.e, pathUSD and MACH (from `channel.least`, 0.05) that covers the amount, else the largest balance. A swarm row is created only when New's Launch is pressed, reused on a retry, and stays hidden from every list until money arrives.
- The swarm page's views are tabs, and each shows only itself (the user's request of 2026-11-02): Transcript, Room (its tab carries who is here and how many watch, `Presence`, instead of a Room header with backers and watching), Ideas (`Ideas.tsx`: the leaderboard of notes with the money behind each and its backers, then every backer with what they put in and how; tapping an idea drafts `@swarm <idea>` in the transcript's composer) and Artifacts, then on a desktop Code, Machines and About. Machines (`Machines.tsx`, `shared/Fleet.ts`, tested in `test/fleet.test.ts`) lists every machine the feed knows: a Modal sandbox from its `compute_create` call (image, CPU, memory, GPU, lease, timeout), a Browserbase browser from `browser_create`, and a contributor's reverse `nc hand` from its join line and `GET /hands` (label, price, what its owner was paid, requests), each with its state and uptime; opening one shows its settings and every command that ran on it (from `hand` on the call), each opening to its output. Since 2026-11-06 (the user's report of a machine showing `volumes [object Object]`), each setting is written out: volumes as `muse at /work (read-only)`, `env` by its names only (never its values), memory in GB, the network as open or blocked, other booleans as yes or no; CPU and memory are left out when a maximum is set, and so is a single GPU's count. A sandbox's commands are the `compute_*` calls that name its `sandboxId`, tied to the machine by `compute_create`'s output (a sandbox started without `attach` is named by that id), shown as `$ <script>` or `write <path>`; a Code Mode cell is no longer listed as a command, since the calls inside it are. A `compute_create` that failed shows as Did not start with the gateway's reason. Checked on swarm f7df8db3fe54's feed: its `h200` sandbox listed 96 commands, `judge` Did not start with the payer's 402, and `probe` showed as stopped. The tab reads `GET /v1/swarms/:id/machines`, which folds the newest 2,000 feed entries (it read the oldest 2,000 before), and falls back to the loaded transcript, where a phone showed only the machines in the last 30 entries (`h200` without `probe` and `judge`).

## How it works

- One operator API key runs every swarm. `SWARMS_GATEWAY_KEY` is a `sk-tempo-` gateway API key that owns every swarm's agent, hands, machines and code repository, each swarm in its own `Gateway-App`, `swarm-<id>`, so swarms never see each other's data. ## API and MCP

- Every operation lives in `Operations` as a function of `(env, input)` returning a `Problem.Result`. The web app's `/api` routes, the public `/v1` routes (`Rest`) and the MCP tools (`Mcp`) are thin adapters over these functions, so validation, errors and side effects match on every surface. That includes transfers (`contribute`, `transfer`), which `/v1` serves under `/v1/swarms/:id/contributions`, and the wallet a swarm from before 0003 gets on its first read. Each operation parses its own input with Zod, and the schemas have no transforms: the MCP SDK validates a tool's arguments with the same schema and passes on the parsed value, so a transforming schema (the pledge amount to atomic units) failed when the operation parsed that value again.
- Every page reads only what the public API serves (the user's request of 2026-11-02, so anyone can build another UI or scrape it): `/v1` has everything `/api` does, plus `GET /v1/problems` (the idea bank, each problem marked open or with the swarm on it), `GET /v1/swarms/:id/machines` (`Fleet.fleet` over the feed and the lent hands: the Machines tab, with each machine's settings and command history) and `GET /v1/swarms/:id/backers` (the Ideas tab: ideas ranked by money and every backer), with MCP tools for each (`problems_list`, `swarm_machines`, `swarm_backers`).
- State machines made explicit so bad states cannot happen: `Swarm.fund` is single-flight (`funding`), so two swaps of one balance never run at once; the Fund component cannot pay again after a payment until Fund again is tapped; Lend your machine is a `Step` union (closed, naming, issuing, failed, issued).
- Pages render on the server and stream (the user's request of 2026-11-02). `src/worker/Page.ts` answers `/`, `/join`, `/new`, `/about`, `/docs` and `/s/:id` for HTML requests (listed in `run_worker_first`). It writes the head of `index.html` at once, then the page's own title, description and Open Graph and X tags, then React's `renderToReadableStream` output of the same `App`. The page's data comes from the same `Operations` the API uses, bounded at 1.5 to 2.5 s so a slow read degrades to client loading: the swarm list, or a swarm's detail and its room. It is followed by `window.__SWARMS_BOOT__` with that data, the config, the server time, the visitor's time zone (cookie `swarms_tz`), whether the device is a phone, and the remembered wallet (cookie `swarms_face`, which `wallet.ts` mirrors from localStorage). The server uses `#ssr` (`src/web/server.tsx`, aliased in `vite.config.ts`), and its components read the request through `boot.ts`: `here()` for the URL, `seeded()` for data (a failure is seeded as `!key`), and the server snapshots of `useMedia`, `useWallet` and `usePath`. `main.tsx` loads the route's module and hydrates (`pages.tsx` keeps the code split while letting the server and hydration render a page without Suspense), so the first paint is the final layout, and polls seeded on the server wait one interval before refetching. Times are formatted in the visitor's zone on both sides. A missing swarm answers 404 with its page. Fixture and storyboard URLs (`?fixture`, `?scene`) stay client-rendered. ## Rooms

- Every swarm has a room where its backers talk (the user's request of 2026-11-01). Anyone reads it; a backer posts. A backer is an address with a counted contribution, or a hand issued while it was signed in (`hands.contributor`, migration 0008; hands issued signed out, over MCP or before 0008 have no owner and make nobody a member). `Store.members` lists them with `backed` (what they put in), `steered` (what of it is behind a note, from a note on the money or from the room) and `available`, the difference.
- Sign-in is accounts' `Handler.auth` (SIWE, the accounts `examples/authentication` pattern) at `/v1/auth`, `/v1/auth/challenge` and `/v1/auth/logout`, with `cookie: false`, so every session is a bearer token, 30 days, and `origin` pinned to `SWARMS_URL`. Its store is the D1 table `sessions` (`Sessions.store`: `take` is `delete … returning`, so a challenge nonce is used once, and expired rows go on every write) rather than the example's Durable Object. The page signs in with one Tempo Wallet prompt: `wallet_connect` with `capabilities.auth = { url: config.auth, returnToken: true }`, keeps `{ address, token }` in localStorage and sends `Authorization: Bearer` on every `/api` request. Funding does not sign anyone in, so sending money never depends on the session handshake. A room that answers `me: null` to a token the page holds signs the page out. A Tempo access key signs in as its wallet too (2026-11-06, the user's request to run everything headless with the wallet's access keys): accounts calls viem's `verifyMessage`, whose Tempo chain refuses a keychain envelope unless `mode: 'allowAccessKey'`, so `Sessions.auth` hands it a client whose chain's `verifyHash` sets that mode, which recovers the access key and checks on the AccountKeychain that it is authorized for the wallet, not expired and not revoked. Before, the admin access key's signature answered 401 `signature does not match address`. Locally with viem, the admin key's signature verified for its wallet, and a key the wallet never authorized and a plain EOA claiming the wallet did not.
- A backer's name in the room is their username. Since 2026-11-05 ("require people to set a username instead of the automatic guest usernames") posting, steering and side questions need one: `Operations.say` and `Operations.btw` answer 403 `username_required` to a signed-in account without a name, and the room's gate and the transcript's composer ask for one (`Username.tsx`) before they show a composer. Someone without a username, a backer who funded without signing in, shows as a short address (`Mention.short`, `0x73dd…c0c0`, written without `@` by `Mention.at`) in the room, the transcript, the contributors and the team roster, and is not offered in @-completion. Before, such a backer got a made name, `Mention.handle(address)`: one of 64 words picked by the address's first four bytes and its last four hex digits (`@quark-1a2b`), which still resolves as a mention in old messages. A signed-in account sets its username with `POST /api/me` or `/v1/me` `{ name }` (the user's request of 2026-11-02; `null` or `""` clears it) and reads it with `GET`, and the header's wallet button shows `@name`. Usernames live in D1 `names` (migration 0009), keyed by lower-case address, unique, stored in lower case, 3 to 24 letters, digits, `-` and `_`, starting with a letter and ending with a letter or digit (`Mention.invalid`). A name shaped like a made one (`word-1a2b`) is refused, so nobody can pass for another backer's made name, and so are `swarm`, `lead`, `all`, `everyone`, `here`, `admin`, `system`, `swarms` and `you`. A taken name is 409 `conflict`; a released name is free at once. Mentions resolve against both names, the username and the made one, when a message is posted, and are stored as addresses; a message's text keeps the name it was written with, so a mention of a username that was later changed no longer highlights. - Audit fixes (2026-11-05, the user's request after an audit of the branch, side-question and hand work: "let's do EVERYTHING you've found"). Four holes were exploited first on production swarm bbff259f5abb (`/tmp/states-e2e/audit.mjs`, `audit2.mjs`): a fresh wallet with nothing in was refused a side question (403), issued a hand without connecting anything (201), showed as a member with `ways: machine` and $0, and then got an answer the swarm paid for; a signed-out caller took a hand slot (201); a $0.01 fund of omega whose message was 30,000 emoji (60,000 characters, 120 KB) was refused by the gateway (`message exceeds the 65536-byte limit`) after its budget had gone from 0.10 to 0.11, while the backer's available stayed 0.30 and the answer said nothing was put behind it; and a wallet that put $0.01 behind omega stopped it, though another backer had $0.05 behind it and the agent gave it $0.10. What changed:
  - Hands: a hand row makes its contributor a member only once the gateway has seen it connected or serving requests (`hands.seen_at`, migration 0017; `Hands.seen` marks it from the gateway's listing when the machines tab, an issuance or the lender's own room post reads it). Issuing stays open to anyone, MCP included, but when a swarm has its 32 hands the oldest that never connected is forgotten and detached at the gateway to make room, as is any that has not connected in a day (`Hands.evictable`), so unused slots can no longer block lending; only 32 connected hands answer 409.
  - Side questions need at least $0.01 of the asker's own money behind the swarm (or a team seat), not just membership; a swarm answers at most 120 an hour from everyone (`Btw.perSwarmHour`, kv `btw:*`, chosen without measurement) besides 20 per asker; a thread is at most 32 KiB of questions and answers (`Btw.maxThreadBytes`; the side pane drops the oldest exchanges to fit, `Btw.fit`), where 20 exchanges of 4,000 and 20,000 characters used to be accepted; and answers are redacted like the transcript.
  - Stopping a branch takes more than half of the money backers put behind it, or an admin; a branch nobody funded is an admin's to stop. Before a team exists the admin is the creator, else the biggest backer.
  - A fund's message is at most 48,000 bytes (`Btw.maxMessageBytes`, under the gateway's 65,536 with the header), and `Swarm.direct` puts the branch's previous budget back when the gateway refuses the message after the budget was raised.
  - A branch with no budget of its own is not capped by funding it: money put behind it, or moved to it, counts as backing (shown, told to it, returned when it stops) and no gateway budget is set, where a $0.01 fund used to cap an unlimited branch at $0.01, a stop that needed no permission.   - Branch money is scoped to the agent: `steers.agent` and `branches.agent` (migration 0017, which rebuilds `branches` with primary key `(swarm, agent, branch)`), because subagent ids restart at 1 when a swarm starts a new agent and the new subagent 1 inherited the old branch 1's backers, base and settlement. Rows from before the migration have agent `''` and are adopted by the swarm's current agent the first time its branches are listed (`Store.adopt`), or released when it starts a new agent first. When `Swarm.lead` creates a new agent, `Swarm.release` returns what backers put behind the old agent's unsettled branches (what each had not spent, from the old agent's listing, or all of it when the old session does not answer) and says so in the room. `direct` and `halt` refuse a call made against an agent the swarm has since replaced.

  - A collapsed side thread's note id hashes the note with the whole thread, so a second thread with the same first question is a separate idea: before, anyone could add their own thread, with answers they wrote, to another backer's note for $0.01 and have the agent read it with that note's full weight.

## Artifacts

- A swarm publishes artifacts so people can follow along without reading the transcript: a live dashboard of its numbers, a chart, a table, a write-up (the user's request of 2026-11-02). They are built on the gateway's existing public storage, so the gateway needed no change: an artifact is files under `artifacts/<slug>/` in the swarm's bucket and a manifest at the kv key `artifacts/<slug>` (`{ title, summary, entry, kind, updated }`), all public, in the swarm's app under the operator wallet. ## Contributor hands

- Anyone can lend a swarm their own machine as a nanocode hand. `POST /v1/swarms/:id/hands` (also `/api`, and the MCP tool `swarm_hand`) takes an optional `label` and answers the hand's `name`, `token` and `url`, plus both ways to attach:
  - `cli`: `nc hand '<url>' --token <token>` (nanocode 0.6.7), and `command`, the gateway's line that installs nanocode first.
  - `library`: a Node module that runs nanocode-ts's `Hand.connect` over its in-memory shell (`Connect.library`, release build-20f97e0, installed with `npm`).
  - `GET /v1/swarms/:id/hands` lists the issued hands and which are connected. The swarm page's "Connect your machine" box (`Connect.tsx`) issues one and shows both forms.
- A hand is an account hand of the operator key in the swarm's app (`POST /v1/hands?name=` with `Gateway-App: swarm-<id>`; see the gateway's AGENTS.md on account hands), so the agent and its subagents can use it in every turn, whichever contribution pays.
- Hands issued before `upgrade`, under a swarm's old pool key, stop attaching, and so do all hands when the operator key changes; contributors issue new ones.
- Hand pricing belongs to the gateway and the hand SDK: an owner prices their own hand (`nc hand <url> --token <t> --pricing prices.json` with `{"payee","describe","rules":[{"paths","ops","usd"}]}`, or nanocode-ts `Hand.connect(…, { pricing: { payee, describe, price(operation) } })`), the gateway's hub charges the agent that made each priced call before it releases the reply (operation `hand.use` on `/mcp`, so this app's payer pays it like any MCP call, at the price as `amount`), and the gateway pays the payee in USDC.e. So Swarms keeps no prices, meters nothing and sends no payouts (the user's decision of 2026-10-31, after a first version that metered and paid hands from the swarm's wallet). It reads `GET /v1/hands`: `requests` (gateway 8c471aa: the operations the hub dispatched, the hand's environment probe included, never counted again on a replay) and, from gateway `feat/paid-hands` (agent 14's branch, not deployed on 2026-10-31), `pricing` (`payee`, `describe`), `charges` (`count`, `usd`, billed to this account's agents), `earned`, `paid` and `cap`. `Hands.terms` turns them into the price summary, charges and earnings that the Connect box, the hands API and the agent's prompt show (`test/hands.test.ts`). At each turn's start `cap` sets every connected hand's cap to a fifth of what is left to spend with `POST /v1/hands/<name>/cap` `{ usd }`, which starts a new window each time, and a hand that joins mid-turn gets the same cap; it sends a cap only for hands whose listing has a `cap` field, so nothing reaches a gateway without caps.
- Lent hands are paid over MPP channels to their owners (2026-10-31, with gateway `feat/mpp-hand-billing`, not deployed that day): for a hand charge the gateway's payer request carries the owner's OUSD session challenge first, with `recipient` (the owner's address) and `hand`, and each channel snapshot names its `payee`. `Swarm.charge` then tries the swarm's wallet on a channel of its own to that owner first (source `hand:<payee>`, its mppx store `mppx:hand:<payee>:`, opened with 0.05 or the price, whichever is more, and topped up by 0.05, smaller than the gateway channel's 0.1 because it can pay only that owner), and only when the wallet cannot (no OUSD left, a USDC.e owner, a refused credential) falls back to the usual sources paying the gateway's challenge, which the gateway turns into its payout to the owner; a MACH pledge always falls back, because MACH cannot reach an owner. `Payer.offer` takes the recipient and now hands mppx a 402 with only the chosen challenge: before, a wallet paying the gateway's OUSD challenge on a hand charge would have signed the owner's OUSD challenge, the first in the list. The owner channels count in `pool_spent`, not in what the swarm may spend, since their deposits pay only their owner. At done `settle` closes them with `requestClose` from the wallet (no cooperative close: the gateway's `kv/get` route takes only its own channels), which the gateway's close supervision answers, and the refund waits for every such channel's deposit to reach 0 (`closing:hand:<payee>`). `Hands.terms` adds `channels` (what the owner was paid over channels and how much settled) and the price's currency, which the Connect box and `Lent` in the OpenAPI document show. ## Development

- `pnpm dev` runs the Worker and the page. Set `SWARMS_STATE=<dir>` to keep local Durable Object and D1 state there, and `SWARMS_GATEWAY=<url>` to use a local gateway: only then, and only under `vite` serve, the Worker's `GATEWAY_URL` is that URL and `global_fetch_strictly_public` is off, because the flag sends fetches to the public internet, which a local gateway is not on. Under `vite` serve the `GATEWAY` service binding is dropped, so development calls the gateway by `fetch`. Builds and deploys keep `wrangler.jsonc` as it is. `pnpm db:migrate:local` (add `--persist-to <dir>` with `SWARMS_STATE`) applies `migrations/`. `.dev.vars` needs `SWARMS_SECRET`, `SWARMS_GATEWAY_KEY` and `SWARMS_URL`, a public URL of the dev server, because the gateway calls the pay route there: `cloudflared tunnel --url http://localhost:<port> --http-host-header localhost` (vite answers only its own host names, so the tunnel rewrites the Host header).
- `pnpm check` type-checks the Worker and the page. `pnpm build` builds both. `pnpm exec wrangler dev -c dist/swarms/wrangler.json` runs the build, where CORS preflights behave as deployed.
- Sign-in in development: the session origin is `SWARMS_URL` and Tempo Wallet's frame fetches `/v1/auth/challenge` from the page's own origin, so sign in on the page served at `SWARMS_URL` (the tunnel), not on localhost. A script can sign in on localhost: the message names `SWARMS_URL`'s host whatever host served it.
