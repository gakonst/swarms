# Swarms

A community-funded science project on the Tempo gateway (https://gateway.tempo-dev.workers.dev): anyone starts a research swarm with a question and evidence requirements, funds it with a one-off transfer, a daily budget or a pledge, and can contribute to an existing swarm or lend a machine. Swarm World makes the research visible. It uses only the gateway's public HTTP API, as any outside developer would, so it also tests that API.

## Research experience

- The home page (`Home.tsx`) is two big boxes, each one link (the user's request of 2026-11-02: two big buttons for a newcomer, not a list): Start a breakthrough (how many ideas wait, and up to four open ideas as small white cards) opens `/new`, and Be part of the future (how many are live or open, and up to four swarms as small cards with their raised amount) opens `/join`. The cards are a preview, not targets; phones show two per box. The lists with tap-to-expand rows live on `/join` and `/new`. `/about` (`About.tsx`, lazy, linked from the header and a swarm's About tab) says what Swarms is in five rows (Start, Fuel, Work, Steer, Finish), three plain facts (public, reported not reviewed, no returns) and links to start, join and the docs. Step headings carry no subtitles; the New flow's steps are Start, Fuel, Launch.
- The idea bank (`problems` in `shared/ResearchBrief.ts`, the user's request of 2026-11-02) is a curated list of problems, each with an `id`, a short label and hint, a title and a full evidence brief. `open(swarms)` drops every problem whose title matches (case-insensitively) a listed swarm that is not done, so the Pick step, the Start door and the `/x` card offer only problems nobody is working on; a finished swarm frees its problem again. The Pick step shows six at a time with More ideas, `/new?q=<id>` opens one on the Fuel step, and when every problem is taken it points to Join. A swarm stores the problem it was started from in `swarms.problem` (migration 0010, backfilled by exact title for older swarms), set by `POST /v1/swarms` and `swarm_create` with `problem` (one of the bank's ids, 400 otherwise) and exposed on `Summary.problem`; the Pick step and the `/x` card send it, a custom idea sends none, and `open` matches on it, so editing the title does not free a problem. Checked locally on 2026-11-02: 0010 applied, a swarm created with `problem: "sat-solver"` answered it back, and an unknown id answered 400.
- Design principle (the user's, 2026-11-02): avoid explicit buttons where the row or card itself can be the click target. The home doors, the join tiles, the idea cards, the notes under Back it (the row backs that note, with a + affordance), the artifact announcements in the room, the swarm's status callout (the whole strip opens Back it or the result), the empty transcript (opens Back it), the empty and offline join list, and the not-found and offline pages (rows: Try again, Join one, Start one) follow it. The join list is compact rows (status dot, one-line title, raised; the status words, area and chevron were dropped at the user's request), about 46 px each, in one column up to 760 px wide (scrolling inside its box on a phone), instead of 168 px cards. They are sorted by status and then creation time, so rows do not jump on each poll. One row is open at a time: the first tap expands it (full title, goal, the latest report's first plain line, what is left, backers, last activity), a tap on another row moves the expansion there, and a tap on the open row goes to the swarm. The New flow's Pick step is the same list (the user's request of 2026-11-02): one row per open idea with its mark and label, More ideas and Your own idea as the last rows; the first tap expands an idea into its hint and its brief in an editable box (the user's request: a template's prompt must be editable), with Fuel it; a second tap on the row also moves to Fuel with the edited brief, and opening another idea replaces the draft with that idea's brief; and Your own idea expands into the question box with Fuel it. Rows sit flush in one bordered group with dividers and no gaps (the user's request of 2026-11-02): the join list, the door ideas and peek, the idea list, the artifact list, the not-found rows, the More sheet and the room roster.
- The app has no footer and no "Demo data" line (the user's request of 2026-11-02). Desktop pages are centred: the two doors are a 640 px-high block centred in the window, Join's list is a 760 px column, New and Docs keep their widths centred with a top offset that grows with the window's height, and not-found pages fill the space under the header and centre in it. `/docs` is reached from `d`, the X cards and links; nothing in the header points to it.
- Funding is one component, `Fund` in `Contribute.tsx` (the user's requests of 2026-11-02: one dollar amount, the currency chosen for you, One time or Monthly, every contribution charged up front, no subtitles, less is more). It is the swarm's title row (with Change on New), a dollar amount with $2, $5 and $10, a One time / Monthly switch and one button (Pay $2.00, Pay $2.00 a month, or Launch · $2.00 on New); a note behind the money shows as a removable chip. A swarm page opens it as a sheet from the head's Fund button, the callout, the empty transcript and the room (a phone sheet from the bottom, a centred card on a desktop), and the Fund tab shows it inline beside the ledger. New uses the same component as its second step (Start, Fund). Errors and confirmations are `Alert` cards (`Alert.tsx`: a mark, a headline, an optional detail, an optional action), with `explain` turning wallet and network errors into plain headlines. The words Back it, Fuel it, Refuel it and Be the first are gone. One time in a stablecoin is a `transferWithMemo` to the swarm's wallet, charged at once; One time in MACH is a pledge, because MACH cannot reach the swarm's wallet (policy 583), and its key's channel opens with the whole remaining limit (`attempt`: `deposit` is `room.amount` for a key source), so the full pledge moves into escrow at its first payment rather than at approval. Monthly is a budget with `period` 2,592,000 s (30 days) and a key valid 365 days (`Store.budget`); `budgeted` runs `fund` at once, and `pull` takes every due budget's whole remaining period limit (less the transfer fee) whenever it runs, while stablecoin pledges are still pulled by need; a used-up budget waits for `periodEnd`. Budgets approved before this keep their daily period. The token is picked by `choose`: the first of OUSD, USDC.e, pathUSD and, for One time, MACH that covers the amount, else the largest balance. A swarm row is created only when New's Launch is pressed, reused on a retry, and stays hidden from every list until money arrives.
- The swarm page's views are tabs, and each shows only itself (the user's request of 2026-11-02): Transcript, Room (its tab carries who is here and how many watch, `Presence`, instead of a Room header with backers and watching), Ideas (`Ideas.tsx`: the leaderboard of notes with the money behind each and its backers, then every backer with what they put in and how; tapping an idea drafts `@swarm <idea>` in the transcript's composer) and Artifacts, then on a desktop Code, Machines and About. Machines (`Machines.tsx`, `shared/Fleet.ts`, tested in `test/fleet.test.ts`) lists every machine the feed knows: a Modal sandbox from its `compute_create` call (image, CPU, memory, GPU, lease, timeout), a Browserbase browser from `browser_create`, and a contributor's reverse `nc hand` from its join line and `GET /hands` (label, price, what its owner was paid, requests), each with its state and uptime; opening one shows its settings and every command that ran on it (from `hand` on the call), each opening to its output.
- The storyboard covers every flow the user has asked for: home, join, start, a swarm's states, the transcript, steering with @swarm (amounts, new money, approval, decline, failure), the room, ideas, fund, artifacts and machines, and the X cards and other pages.
- Steering is a leaderboard, not a funding form (the user's request of 2026-11-02: copy-trade an idea). Tapping an idea, a message's $ chip or a $ row in the transcript drafts `@swarm <idea>` in the transcript's composer, which shows $0.50, $1, $5 or your own amount when there is text. Every steer is new money (the user's decision of 2026-11-02: what a backer put in when starting or with a monthly budget is separate, and each follow-on message pays again): sending first transfers the chosen amount as a one-off stablecoin transfer (one Tempo Wallet approval, token picked by `choose`), then posts the steer with exactly that amount behind it. An @swarm message typed in the room drafts it in the transcript's composer instead of moving existing backing. The swarm has no Fund tab any more; the head's Fund button and `?fund=` links raise the Fund card in the transcript.
- Funding lives in the transcript's flow (the user's request of 2026-11-02): the head's Fund button, the composer's $ button and every other Fund entry switch to the transcript and raise the Fund card from the bottom of the transcript pane in place of the composer (a tray, closed with × or after paying); the Fund tab keeps it inline beside the ledger. - The transcript has a composer (the user's request of 2026-11-02: it should feel like a multiplayer room). What a backer types there is posted to the room as `@swarm <text>` with up to $0.50 of their unassigned backing behind it (`Api.say` with `steer`), so it steers the agent like the room's @swarm; signed-out visitors and non-backers see a Steer the agent row that signs in or opens Fund. Room messages that steer (`Mention.steers`) are woven into the transcript by time as speech bubbles under their @handle; other room chatter stays in the room. People → opens the room (the Room tab under 1100 px, the room's composer beside it on a desktop). The room shares its messages, members and the visitor with the swarm page through `onMessages`, so the transcript needs no second socket.
- A swarm's head is one line (the user's request of 2026-11-02): a status dot (its title says Live, Paused, Not started or Finished), the title on one line, what it has raised and one button, Fund, or Result once it is done. The meta line, the Raised / Spent / Left / Backers figures and the status callout with its Why? are gone; the money is under the Fund tab, and why a swarm waits is the feed's own status line.

- A swarm is never done (2026-11-03, the user's request: "I can always keep steering a swarm, a swarm just produces artifacts and git code as it goes"): the prompt no longer asks for STATUS: done, a turn's answer never ends the swarm, and contributions, budgets, pledges, steers and hands are no longer refused for a done swarm. ## How it works

- One operator API key runs every swarm. `SWARMS_GATEWAY_KEY` is a `sk-tempo-` gateway API key that owns every swarm's agent, hands, machines and code repository, each swarm in its own `Gateway-App`, `swarm-<id>`, so swarms never see each other's data. It pays for none of a swarm's work: the agent is created with an agent payer (`POST /v1/agents` with `payer: { url: <SWARMS_URL>/api/swarms/<id>/pay, secret }`, tempoxyz/gateway 82d56b6), so the gateway asks this app to pay for every model call, MCP tool call (`compute_*`, `git_*`, `kv_*`) and subagent request the agent makes. A swarm keeps one agent for its whole life; `lead` creates it when there is none or the gateway answers 404, and `PATCH /v1/agents/:id` moves the payer when `SWARMS_URL` changes. The operator key pays only the app's own requests: `compute/list` and `compute/terminate` when a swarm that pauses or finishes leaves machines running. On 2026-10-31 the operator key was created from the test wallet 0x9759…e122 by its admin key with the gateway checkout's `.scratch/admin-install.mjs` (`REQUEST='{"limit":2,"days":365,"deposit":0.1,"topUp":0.1}'`): access key 0x34e15c9ba6ea1a7061b6077a6520adcc9e298b8d, installed in 0xdd043fd77d3cbaff1df73e37a16c62ee0e698a180211445bf9cedf3d998362dc, expiring 2027-10-31. Agents expire with their key, so a new operator key means new agents, each primed with the last three updates. After three local swarms its `/api/keys/me` still showed no channel and its whole 2 MACH limit: it had paid for nothing.
- Every contribution is money or a key the app holds, and contributors fund the same three ways as before:
  - Send once: a `transferWithMemo` of USDC.e, pathUSD or OUSD from any wallet to the swarm's wallet, then `POST /api/swarms/:id/contributions` with its `hash` and an optional `note`. The Worker reads the receipt and counts the TIP-20 `Transfer`s to the swarm whose `from` is the transaction's sender (so a swap's or a channel close's transfers into the wallet are not contributions); the contributor is that sender. A note counts only when a `TransferWithMemo` to the swarm carries `keccak256("note:<swarm>:<note>")` (`Memo.memo`; without a note the page sends `keccak256("swarm:<swarm>")`), so nobody can attach a note to someone else's transfer; a hash counted without a note can get its note later, and each hash counts once (409 after).
  - Daily budget ("X a day"): `POST /api/swarms/:id/budgets` with `token` (usdce, pathusd or ousd), `amount` per day (at least `minPledge`) and an optional `note` generates a secp256k1 access key, kept sealed in `budgets`, and answers its `authorizeAccessKey`: a periodic limit of `amount` with `period` 86,400 s, the token's `transferWithMemo` scoped to `recipients: [swarm wallet]`, 30 days. Tempo Wallet approves it with `wallet_connect` (nanocode-ts machines' pattern, with `keyType: secp256k1`), and `POST /api/swarms/:id/budgets/:budget` verifies the signed authorization against the request (key, chain, expiry, token, period, limit at most the request, scopes) and stores it; the first pull carries it on Tempo. A script whose approver is an admin access key sends instead a signed transaction carrying the authorization, which the Worker broadcasts (the approver pays its fee): on 2026-10-30 a pull whose own transaction carried an admin-signed authorization was refused at `prepareTransactionRequest` with `An internal error was received`.
  - Pledge: `POST /api/swarms/:id/pledges` with `amount`, `token` (MACH by default, or OUSD, USDC.e or pathUSD) and an optional `note` generates a secp256k1 access key the same way, capped at exactly the pledge for 7 days, and stores it in `budgets` with `period` 0: the table holds every key the app holds, and a period of 0 is a one-off cap. A MACH pledge's key may only call the TIP-1034 escrow's `open`, `topUp`, `requestClose` and `withdraw` and pays the gateway itself over its own MACH session channel. A pledge in a stablecoin is a pull key like a budget without a period: the swarm pulls from it into its wallet as the work needs money, and records each pull as a `pledge` contribution, which counts for refunds but not again for `raised` or notes, because the pledge counted its whole amount when it was approved. `POST /api/swarms/:id/pledges/:pledge` approves it with the `keyAuthorization` or a signed transaction (`Keys.approve`, shared with budgets) and refuses a wallet that holds less than the pledge (409 `insufficient_balance`). Pledges made on main before this were gateway API keys, which can pay only for their own agent; the first step of the new code revokes them (`upgrade`), which closes their channels and returns what they did not spend.
- MACH cannot be pooled. MACH's TIP-403 policy (compound policy 583: any sender, recipients from whitelist 582, any mint recipient) lets MACH reach only whitelisted merchants such as the gateway's recipient and the escrow. A `transferWithMemo` from a funded wallet to a fresh swarm address reverted with `PolicyForbids`, and the MACH swapper's `swapTo` reverted for any recipient but a merchant (checked on Tempo mainnet on 2026-10-30). USDC.e (policy 1), pathUSD and OUSD (policy 2) can reach a fresh address. That is why a MACH pledge pays over its own channel while stablecoins are pulled into the swarm's wallet.
- Every swarm has its own wallet: a secp256k1 key generated when the swarm is created (or on first read for older rows), AES-GCM-sealed with `SWARMS_SECRET` in `swarms.sealed_wallet`, its address in `swarms.wallet`. It swaps the USDC.e and pathUSD it receives to OUSD on the stablecoin DEX (`swapExactAmountIn` with `minAmountOut` 0.5% under `getSellQuote`, paying the fee in the input token and leaving 0.002 of it) and pays the gateway from its own OUSD session channel, signed by its root key. Measured on 2026-10-30: 0.1 pathUSD quoted 0.1 OUSD (fee 0.000052 to 0.000351 per swap, 584,841 gas). Before each turn, and during one whenever less than `reserve` + `headroom` (0.1) is payable or the wallet holds unswapped stablecoins, `fund` fills the wallet up to `stock` (the reserve plus three times `headroom`) from pull keys: it reads each active budget's and stablecoin pledge's `getRemainingLimit` (remaining and `periodEnd`), `getMetadata` (revoked) and the wallet's balance, takes the least recently pulled, one per 0.03 still needed, and splits the need among them. Each pull is a `transferWithMemo` (`keccak256("budget:<swarm>:<key>")`) signed by the key, paying its fee in its own token inside its limit: on 2026-10-30 a 0.01 pathUSD pull used 304,090 gas and 0.000183 of fee, a transfer to any other address answered `CallNotAllowed`, and one past the remaining limit `SpendingLimitExceeded`. A budget used up for its period waits until `periodEnd`, one whose wallet holds too little waits 15 minutes, a used-up pledge is `spent`, and a revoked or expired key stops; the page shows each budget's next pull time.
- The payer is `POST /api/swarms/:id/pay` (`Operations.pay`, then `Swarm.pay`, one request at a time per swarm). It answers only the bearer secret the swarm generated for its agent (64 hex, in the Durable Object, compared in constant time) and only for the swarm's own app, and signs only a `tempo` session challenge to the gateway's recipient 0x40def0Cf3B5d28B6e9a7976BA10591e515AfECFE on chain 4217 for at most $1 (`Chain.cap`, which is what `git_create` costs), or, for a lent hand's charge, an OUSD session challenge to the owner the gateway names in `recipient` (below), so a leaked secret buys gateway requests and hand operations from this swarm's money and nothing else. Its sources are the swarm's wallet (OUSD) and each active MACH key; it tries the wallet first, because its money already left the contributors' wallets and goes back only through refund transfers, then the key that paid last, then the rest by what they can pay now. Each source has its own mppx session client and channel in the object's storage (`mppx:<source>:`, `channel:<source>`). A channel opens with 0.1 or the request's price, whichever is more, bounded by what the source can put in (the wallet's OUSD, or a key's `getRemainingLimit` bounded by the wallet's balance). A voucher is the larger of the gateway's `requiredCumulative`, one past the highest voucher still in flight (signed in the last 10 s and above the accepted one) or else the accepted one, and `spent` plus this source's vouchers not yet accepted plus the price (without a snapshot, one past the last voucher signed); before 2026-10-31 it was always at least one past the last voucher signed, so every voucher the gateway refused or that crossed a refund raised the floor for good and the deposit had to cover it, the arithmetic the gateway's payer contract recommends, so refunded reservations are not paid twice; when the deposit is short the answer carries a `topUp` of at least 0.1 beside the voucher. A source that cannot pay is skipped for this request when its channel still holds a call's reservation (0.04, so it is only busy) or it could pay a smaller request, and rests otherwise: a budget until `periodEnd`, a wallet short of the token for 15 minutes, anything else for 10; a key that is revoked, expired or out of limit ends. A source whose credential the gateway refused on its third attempt rests, and a channel the gateway reports `closing` or refused with 410 is dropped so the next payment opens another. A source whose channel lacks deposit is also busy for 90 s after it last paid, because the shortfall is then mostly other calls' reservations, which the gateway refunds when they end: on 2026-11-01 a web search reserved about 0.18 (0.09 once the SDK sent `max_tool_calls`), two subagents searching at once held all of swarm 48ba542731e3's 0.4, and the next model call was answered `limit_reached`, which rested the wallet for 10 minutes and paused the swarm with 0.37 of it about to come back. A channel with a call's reservation (0.04) unspent counts as busy only until 10 minutes after its source last paid (`heldMs`): before 2026-11-02 it counted as busy for good, and swarm 06cf5d7c8f92's 2 MACH pledge, whose backer's wallet held 0 MACH so it could add nothing, kept 0.04 or more unspent but less than its next model call's reservation, with nothing else in flight; the payer answered 429 busy from 15:09 to past 16:18 UTC while the harness retried that one call every 15 s, and the turn never ended, so the swarm never paused. When the payer answers `limit_reached` because no single source can pay a request whose price it knows, it records that price (`wanted`); a failed turn then pauses the swarm as waiting for money rather than counting toward the three-error rest, and no turn starts until one source can pay that much (`costly` is the reason shown); a completed turn forgets it. Without it, that swarm, its wallet holding $0.003 and its pledge's channel $0.21 that could not grow, failed three turns, rested and woke at the wallet's 10-minute rest, which cost $0.0006 and posted two room messages every 10 minutes (16:26 to 16:28 UTC on 2026-11-02). When the only sources left are busy the answer is 429 with `retry-after: 2`, which the harness retries; otherwise 402 `limit_reached`, which fails the turn's request, and a turn that failed with less than `reserve` payable pauses the swarm with the amount and the time a resting source or budget comes back (its alarm). New money wakes it. After a turn the object pays one `kv/get` (0.0001) from each source with a channel to read `spent` from its receipt, because the gateway reports `spent` only with each ask, and it includes reservations it refunds after the call.
- A turn (`steer: false`) starts when the sources can pay at least `reserve` (0.05) now, together. While it runs the object pages `GET /v1/agents/:id/events` every 2 s into its feed and every 10 s reads the turn, the sources and `GET /v1/hands`. Nothing limits a turn's length or the number of turns, so a swarm runs until its agent reports the goal met (`STATUS: done`) or its money is gone. Three failed turns in a row pause it, except turns that failed because the money ran out. Sandboxes the agent left running are terminated when the swarm pauses or finishes (`sweep`), not between turns. Before 2026-11-02 a swarm worked in rounds, one turn each, and a round got one explorer per `perWorker` (0.1) beyond the reserve, up to 3; every round's end terminated the sandboxes.
- Contributors steer with money. A transfer, budget or pledge may carry a note; contributions with the same note (normalized) back it together, and a note's weight is everything behind it, pledged, sent or pulled from a budget, stablecoins at par. A note that arrives during a turn steers it (`steer: true`) with its weight and its share of everything contributed, and every continuation lists the notes by weight.
- Done: `done` terminates leftover sandboxes, disconnects the issued hands and ends every budget and pledge (their keys stay valid on chain until the contributor revokes them in Tempo Wallet or they expire; the swarm never spends them again). `settle` then closes every channel: first with a cooperative `close` credential to `kv/get` (for a contributor's key the `CloseAuthorization` is signed with the raw access key, because mppx wraps an access key's signature in a keychain envelope that TIP-1034 refuses), which closes and refunds at once but needs the gateway's session methods to hold a settlement `account` (tempoxyz/gateway 70818b8, not on gateway main on 2026-10-31, where every close credential failed); then with `requestClose` from the channel's signer, its fee paid by the swarm's wallet as Tempo fee payer (the gateway's own `Keys.close` pattern), which the gateway's close supervision answers within about a minute, capturing what was spent and returning the rest to the channel's payer. A MACH key cannot pay its own fee (MACH has no Fee AMM liquidity), so a swarm whose wallet holds no stablecoin cannot close a MACH pledge's channel until the gateway holds the settlement account; it retries for 30 minutes and then logs it. Once the wallet's channel has closed on chain (`getChannelState` deposit 0, or after 20 minutes) the swarm sends what its wallet holds, less 0.004 for fees, back to its transfer, budget and pledge-pull contributors in proportion to what each put in.
- Upgrading a swarm from main (`upgrade`, the first step that finds no payer secret): it revokes the swarm's old OUSD gateway key, whose channel closes into the swarm's wallet, revokes every pledge that was a gateway API key, cancels a round that was running on the old agent, forgets the old agent and pool state, and the next round creates the payer agent, primed with the last three reports. The old key's channel closes into the swarm's wallet within about a minute, so `upgrade` sets `returning` two minutes ahead and `wake` counts it: the first version of this branch paused such a swarm with $0 payable and waited for new money. A swarm that finished on main but had not refunded yet waits for main's `expected` balance as before. Hands issued under the old pool key stop attaching; contributors issue new ones. Checked on 2026-10-31 by running main (0cbafe8) and then this branch on one local state: swarm ebcd9ecbe7bd ran 11 rounds on main's pool key, then this code's first step revoked that key, cancelled round 11 on the old agent and, after a 0.02 pathUSD transfer woke it (before `returning`), created a payer agent that ran rounds 12 to 30 from the swarm's wallet, which now held the old channel's money, until a steering note ended it; its wallet's channel closed (0x47f75e52eb07423357cc4b3c74c9e58b8f427c4334baf68227a032c79ecd3ae3) and 0.150844 OUSD went back (0xe3923483d87f21858abddb9b1c16676a59f794d969c4d78406bf1d54c1056d3f). Swarm 9489bd076e57, done on main, refunded on main before the switch.
- Code: a swarm's code lives in a public repository on the gateway's `/v1/git` (code.storage over MPP), in its own app and owned by the operator key, when the agent decides to make one with `git_create` (the swarm's money pays the $1 through the payer). Nothing in the swarm's messages asks for one since 2026-11-03 ("remove the (2) stuff"): the repository instructions, the $1.04/$1.19 launch gate (`Store.repoAt`, `Store.launch`, pause reason `repo`) and the $2 floor for pledges and budgets that existed so one pledge could pay for the repository are gone; `Store.minPledge` is back at 0.25. Before, from 2026-11-02, the prompt asked for the repository first, as soon as one source could pay $1.04, and no swarm started until $1.19 was payable. The URL carries a token, so every feed entry, report and live delta is redacted (`https://***@`, and any `eyJ…` JWT becomes `[redacted]`). The app reads the repository without a key through the gateway's public routes (`GET /p/<operator wallet>/swarm-<id>/git[/<name>/log|files|read|diff]`, gateway 5b5c47d): after each turn it takes the first public repository of the app (an agent once named one `wc` instead of the name it was given), records the head before the turn as the update's `base` and the commits after it as its `commits`, and the update's feed line names them. ## API and MCP

- Every operation lives in `Operations` as a function of `(env, input)` returning a `Problem.Result`. The web app's `/api` routes, the public `/v1` routes (`Rest`) and the MCP tools (`Mcp`) are thin adapters over these functions, so validation, errors and side effects match on every surface. That includes the transfers and daily budgets above (`contribute`, `budget`, `allow`), which `/v1` serves under `/v1/swarms/:id/contributions` and `/v1/swarms/:id/budgets`, and the wallet a swarm from before 0003 gets on its first read. Each operation parses its own input with Zod, and the schemas have no transforms: the MCP SDK validates a tool's arguments with the same schema and passes on the parsed value, so a transforming schema (the pledge amount to atomic units) failed when the operation parsed that value again.
- Every page reads only what the public API serves (the user's request of 2026-11-02, so anyone can build another UI or scrape it): `/v1` has everything `/api` does, plus `GET /v1/problems` (the idea bank, each problem marked open or with the swarm on it), `GET /v1/swarms/:id/machines` (`Fleet.fleet` over the feed and the lent hands: the Machines tab, with each machine's settings and command history) and `GET /v1/swarms/:id/backers` (the Ideas tab: ideas ranked by money and every backer), with MCP tools for each (`problems_list`, `swarm_machines`, `swarm_backers`).
- State machines made explicit so bad states cannot happen: `Swarm.fund` is single-flight (`funding`), because `budgeted` now pulls a month at once and could run beside a round's `advance`, and a second pull of the same budget would be refused and mark it used up; the Fund component cannot pay again after a payment until Fund again is tapped; Lend your machine is a `Step` union (closed, naming, issuing, failed, issued).
- Pages render on the server and stream (the user's request of 2026-11-02). `src/worker/Page.ts` answers `/`, `/join`, `/new`, `/about`, `/docs` and `/s/:id` for HTML requests (listed in `run_worker_first`). It writes the head of `index.html` at once, then the page's own title, description and Open Graph and X tags, then React's `renderToReadableStream` output of the same `App`. The page's data comes from the same `Operations` the API uses, bounded at 1.5 to 2.5 s so a slow read degrades to client loading: the swarm list, or a swarm's detail and its room. It is followed by `window.__SWARMS_BOOT__` with that data, the config, the server time, the visitor's time zone (cookie `swarms_tz`), whether the device is a phone, and the remembered wallet (cookie `swarms_face`, which `wallet.ts` mirrors from localStorage). The server uses `#ssr` (`src/web/server.tsx`, aliased in `vite.config.ts`), and its components read the request through `boot.ts`: `here()` for the URL, `seeded()` for data (a failure is seeded as `!key`), and the server snapshots of `useMedia`, `useWallet` and `usePath`. `main.tsx` loads the route's module and hydrates (`pages.tsx` keeps the code split while letting the server and hydration render a page without Suspense), so the first paint is the final layout, and polls seeded on the server wait one interval before refetching. ## Rooms

- Every swarm has a room where its backers talk (the user's request of 2026-11-01). Anyone reads it; a backer posts. A backer is an address with an approved pledge, a counted transfer or pull, an approved daily budget, or a hand issued while it was signed in (`hands.contributor`, migration 0008; hands issued signed out, over MCP or before 0008 have no owner and make nobody a member). `Store.members` lists them with `backed` (what they put in, counted as `raised` counts it: pledges at their amount, transfers and pulls, not a daily budget's limit), `steered` (what of it is behind a note, from a note on the money or from the room) and `available`, the difference.
- Sign-in is accounts' `Handler.auth` (SIWE, the accounts `examples/authentication` pattern) at `/v1/auth`, `/v1/auth/challenge` and `/v1/auth/logout`, with `cookie: false`, so every session is a bearer token, 30 days, and `origin` pinned to `SWARMS_URL`. Its store is the D1 table `sessions` (`Sessions.store`: `take` is `delete … returning`, so a challenge nonce is used once, and expired rows go on every write) rather than the example's Durable Object. The page signs in with one Tempo Wallet prompt: `wallet_connect` with `capabilities.auth = { url: config.auth, returnToken: true }`, keeps `{ address, token }` in localStorage and sends `Authorization: Bearer` on every `/api` request. Funding does not sign anyone in, so a pledge's approval never depends on the session handshake. A room that answers `me: null` to a token the page holds signs the page out.
- A backer's name in the room is their username, or else `Mention.handle(address)`: one of 64 words picked by the address's first four bytes and its last four hex digits (`@quark-1a2b`). A signed-in account sets its username with `POST /api/me` or `/v1/me` `{ name }` (the user's request of 2026-11-02; `null` or `""` goes back to the made name) and reads it with `GET`; the room's footer and gate have Pick a username / Rename, and the header's wallet button shows `@name`. Usernames live in D1 `names` (migration 0009), keyed by lower-case address, unique, stored in lower case, 3 to 24 letters, digits, `-` and `_`, starting with a letter and ending with a letter or digit (`Mention.invalid`). A name shaped like a made one (`word-1a2b`) is refused, so nobody can pass for another backer's made name, and so are `swarm`, `lead`, `all`, `everyone`, `here`, `admin`, `system`, `swarms` and `you`. A taken name is 409 `conflict`; a released name is free at once. Mentions resolve against both names, the username and the made one, when a message is posted, and are stored as addresses; a message's text keeps the name it was written with, so a mention of a username that was later changed no longer highlights. The page highlights messages that mention the reader.
- `@swarm` steers. A message with it must carry `steer`, an amount of the author's `available` backing that goes behind the rest of the message (`Mention.idea`, 3 to 280 characters) as a note: a row in D1 `steers`, inserted only when the author's available backing covers it, in one statement, so two concurrent steers cannot spend the same backing (409 `insufficient_balance`). `Store.notes` adds `steers` to the other notes, so a room steer weighs like a note on money, merges with the same note's money (`noteId` normalizes case and spaces), counts its author as a backer of the note, is indexed for search and is in every continuation prompt. While a turn runs `Swarm.say` steers it at once (`Swarm.steer`, returning the note it sent, `Steer.live` true); between turns it wakes the swarm, whose next continuation lists it. It moves weight, not money; to put more behind an idea than their backing allows, the page sends the backer to Back it with the idea as the note. Plain messages never reach the agent.
- The room lives in the swarm's Durable Object (table `chat`): messages with author (null for the swarm's own), mentions and steer. The object announces money as it comes in (pledges, transfers and daily budgets, not pulls), when the agent starts or gets back to work, each update (its start) and the end of the swarm. `GET /api/swarms/:id/chat` (and `/v1`) answers the newest 100 messages, `after` or `before` an id, the members and, with a token, `me`; `POST` posts (401 `unauthorized`, 403 `not_member`). `/chat/live` is a hibernating WebSocket (`acceptWebSocket`, `ping` answered by the runtime with `pong`): it sends every new message, presence (signed-in backers here and everyone watching) and typing; a client sends `{type: hello, token}` to be counted, which the object checks against `sessions`. The page reconnects after 3 s, catches up with `after` on every open, and polls every 15 s besides. The `/v1` socket is routed before `Rest`'s CORS middleware, which would wrap the 101. MCP's `swarm_chat` reads a room.

## Artifacts

- A swarm publishes artifacts so people can follow along without reading the transcript: a live dashboard of its numbers, a chart, a table, a write-up (the user's request of 2026-11-02). They are built on the gateway's existing public storage, so the gateway needed no change: an artifact is files under `artifacts/<slug>/` in the swarm's bucket and a manifest at the kv key `artifacts/<slug>` (`{ title, summary, entry, kind, updated }`), all public, in the swarm's app under the operator wallet. The agent writes them with `bucket_put` and `kv_set` ($0.005 and $0.0005, paid like every call); the prompt (`shelving`) explains the convention in full on an agent's first turn and when the swarm first learns of artifacts, lists the published ones in every continuation, and asks to keep a few current rather than publish many. A kv manifest gives a public, paged list by prefix, which the gateway has no bucket equivalent of (its `/p` lists only kv), and a version that grows with each update.
- `GET /api/swarms/:id/artifacts` (and `/v1`, MCP `swarm_artifacts`) lists them: `Artifacts.list` reads `/p/<operator>/swarm-<id>/kv?prefix=artifacts/` (up to three pages, cached 15 s per isolate) and `shared/Artifact.ts` turns each manifest into `{ slug, title, summary, kind, entry, form, version, updatedAt, url }`. A slug is `[a-z0-9][a-z0-9._-]{0,63}`; an entry is at most four segments of `[A-Za-z0-9][A-Za-z0-9._-]*`, so `..`, absolute paths and other escapes fall back to `index.html`. The form follows the entry's extension: page (HTML), image (SVG, PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP), markdown, table (CSV, TSV), json or text. The operator wallet comes from `/api/keys/me` once per isolate (`Gateway.wallet`).
- Files are served from Swarms' own origin at `/a/<swarm>/<slug>/<path>` (in `run_worker_first`), proxied from the gateway's `/p/.../bucket/...` with `If-None-Match` and `Range` passed on. The gateway's `/p` answers every object with `Content-Security-Policy: sandbox`, which blocks scripts, so an HTML dashboard cannot run there. `/a` replaces it with `sandbox allow-scripts allow-popups allow-popups-to-escape-sandbox allow-downloads allow-modals` (never `allow-same-origin`, so the page has an opaque origin and cannot read the site's localStorage, where the session token lives, or its parent) plus `default-src 'none'`, scripts and styles from the swarm's origin, the gateway and four CDNs (jsDelivr, unpkg, cdnjs, esm.sh), `connect-src` and images from the swarm's origin and the gateway only, `frame-ancestors` the swarm's origin and `nosniff`. The page loads its own data files by relative URL (the CSP's origin rule covers `/a`), and can read the swarm's public API.
- The Artifacts tab (`Artifacts.tsx`) lists them and shows the chosen one (`?artifact=<slug>` in the URL): HTML in a sandboxed iframe with the same flags, an image as an image, Markdown rendered with relative links resolved against its folder, CSV and TSV and arrays of JSON objects as tables, other JSON pretty-printed. Reload adds a cache-busting nonce, Expand fills the screen (Escape closes it), Open opens the file. The page polls the list every 30 s while the agent works and every 60 s otherwise. The swarm's Durable Object reads the list at most every 30 s while a turn runs and at its end (`publish`): a new artifact is logged in the feed and announced in the room as a message with an Open button (`Said.artifact`, the `chat.artifact` column), and a new version in a later turn is logged.

## Contributor hands

- Anyone can lend a swarm their own machine as a nanocode hand. `POST /v1/swarms/:id/hands` (also `/api`, and the MCP tool `swarm_hand`) takes an optional `label` and answers the hand's `name`, `token` and `url`, plus both ways to attach:
  - `cli`: `nc hand '<url>' --token <token>` (nanocode 0.6.7), and `command`, the gateway's line that installs nanocode first.
  - `library`: a Node module that runs nanocode-ts's `Hand.connect` over its in-memory shell (`Connect.library`, release build-20f97e0, installed with `npm`).
  - `GET /v1/swarms/:id/hands` lists the issued hands and which are connected. The swarm page's "Connect your machine" box (`Connect.tsx`) issues one and shows both forms.
- A hand is an account hand of the operator key in the swarm's app (`POST /v1/hands?name=` with `Gateway-App: swarm-<id>`; see the gateway's AGENTS.md on account hands), so the agent and its subagents can use it in every turn, whichever contribution pays.
- Hands issued before `upgrade`, under a swarm's old pool key, stop attaching, and so do all hands when the operator key changes; contributors issue new ones.
- Hand pricing belongs to the gateway and the hand SDK: an owner prices their own hand (`nc hand <url> --token <t> --pricing prices.json` with `{"payee","describe","rules":[{"paths","ops","usd"}]}`, or nanocode-ts `Hand.connect(…, { pricing: { payee, describe, price(operation) } })`), the gateway's hub charges the agent that made each priced call before it releases the reply (operation `hand.use` on `/mcp`, so this app's payer pays it like any MCP call, at the price as `amount`), and the gateway pays the payee in USDC.e. So Swarms keeps no prices, meters nothing and sends no payouts (the user's decision of 2026-10-31, after a first version that metered and paid hands from the swarm's wallet). It reads `GET /v1/hands`: `requests` (gateway 8c471aa: the operations the hub dispatched, the hand's environment probe included, never counted again on a replay) and, from gateway `feat/paid-hands` (agent 14's branch, not deployed on 2026-10-31), `pricing` (`payee`, `describe`), `charges` (`count`, `usd`, billed to this account's agents), `earned`, `paid` and `cap`. `Hands.terms` turns them into the price summary, charges and earnings that the Connect box, the hands API and the agent's prompt show (`test/hands.test.ts`). At each turn's start `cap` sets every connected hand's cap to a fifth of what is left to spend with `POST /v1/hands/<name>/cap` `{ usd }`, which starts a new window each time, and a hand that joins mid-turn gets the same cap; it sends a cap only for hands whose listing has a `cap` field, so nothing reaches a gateway without caps.
- Lent hands are paid over MPP channels to their owners (2026-10-31, with gateway `feat/mpp-hand-billing`, not deployed that day): for a hand charge the gateway's payer request carries the owner's OUSD session challenge first, with `recipient` (the owner's address) and `hand`, and each channel snapshot names its `payee`. `Swarm.charge` then tries the swarm's wallet on a channel of its own to that owner first (source `hand:<payee>`, its mppx store `mppx:hand:<payee>:`, opened with 0.05 or the price, whichever is more, and topped up by 0.05, smaller than the gateway channel's 0.1 because it can pay only that owner), and only when the wallet cannot (no OUSD left, a USDC.e owner, a refused credential) falls back to the usual sources paying the gateway's challenge, which the gateway turns into its payout to the owner; a MACH pledge always falls back, because MACH cannot reach an owner. `Payer.offer` takes the recipient and now hands mppx a 402 with only the chosen challenge: before, a wallet paying the gateway's OUSD challenge on a hand charge would have signed the owner's OUSD challenge, the first in the list. The owner channels count in `pool_spent`, not in what the swarm may spend, since their deposits pay only their owner. At done `settle` closes them with `requestClose` from the wallet (no cooperative close: the gateway's `kv/get` route takes only its own channels), which the gateway's close supervision answers, and the refund waits for every such channel's deposit to reach 0 (`closing:hand:<payee>`). `Hands.terms` adds `channels` (what the owner was paid over channels and how much settled) and the price's currency, which the Connect box and `Lent` in the OpenAPI document show. The fallback through a MACH pledge was not run.

## Development

- `pnpm dev` runs the Worker and the page. Set `SWARMS_STATE=<dir>` to keep local Durable Object and D1 state there, and `SWARMS_GATEWAY=<url>` to use a local gateway: only then, and only under `vite` serve, the Worker's `GATEWAY_URL` is that URL and `global_fetch_strictly_public` is off, because the flag sends fetches to the public internet, which a local gateway is not on. Builds and deploys keep `wrangler.jsonc` as it is. `pnpm db:migrate:local` (add `--persist-to <dir>` with `SWARMS_STATE`) applies `migrations/`. `.dev.vars` needs `SWARMS_SECRET`, `SWARMS_GATEWAY_KEY` and `SWARMS_URL`, a public URL of the dev server, because the gateway calls the pay route there: `cloudflared tunnel --url http://localhost:<port> --http-host-header localhost` (vite answers only its own host names, so the tunnel rewrites the Host header).
- `pnpm check` type-checks the Worker and the page. `pnpm build` builds both. `pnpm exec wrangler dev -c dist/swarms/wrangler.json` runs the build, where CORS preflights behave as deployed.
- Sign-in in development: the session origin is `SWARMS_URL` and Tempo Wallet's frame fetches `/v1/auth/challenge` from the page's own origin, so sign in on the page served at `SWARMS_URL` (the tunnel), not on localhost. A script can sign in on localhost: the message names `SWARMS_URL`'s host whatever host served it.
- Deploy: set `SWARMS_SECRET` and `SWARMS_GATEWAY_KEY` with `wrangler secret put`, then `pnpm db:migrate` (0008 adds `sessions`, `steers` and `hands.contributor`; 0009 adds `names`), then `pnpm deploy`. The gateway must have agent payers (82d56b6) and public reads (5b5c47d); cooperative closes need its session account (70818b8). The Worker reaches the gateway over the public internet (`global_fetch_strictly_public`), as a third party would.
